This is the first feature in Markedsdrevet Vedlikehold, a new internal tool for Akershus Energi that digitizes and structures the coordination between two teams — Drift (operations) and Energihandel (energy trading) — when planning maintenance stops at the company's hydropower plants. Today this coordination happens through phone calls and emails, without a shared record or systematic view of market timing. The business needs a structured digital handoff: Drift registers a maintenance stop request with the minimum information Energihandel needs, Energihandel analyzes it against price and water flow forecasts and proposes optimal timing, Drift selects a timing and the decision is logged for both parties. This first feature covers only the Drift side of the initial submission: the registration form and the confirmation screen that follows. All subsequent steps in the workflow — Energihandel's analysis tool, the proposal screen, Drift's selection of a timing, and the final joint confirmation — are separate features that will follow the pattern established here.

The sole actor in this feature is the driftsleder (operations manager) at one of AE's hydropower plants. The driftsleder's job is to identify that a maintenance stop is needed on a specific generating unit and to communicate that need — with just enough detail — to Energihandel so that trading can assess the market impact and propose good timing. The driftsleder is not a power user; the tool must work without training beyond a brief introduction, and it will be used from a desktop in an office or meeting room context.

The driftsleder opens the tool and sees an empty form. The form is organized into three groups of fields. The first group identifies the plant and unit: the driftsleder selects a stasjon (power station) from a list of AE's stations, then selects an aggregat (specific generating unit) within that station. The aggregat selection is dependent on the station — only the aggregats belonging to the selected station are available. The capacity in MW is associated with the aggregat and does not need to be entered manually; it is a property of the aggregat known to the system.

The second group captures the stop itself: a desired start period (a date or rough date range, not a precise timestamp — this is the driftsleder's preference, not a commitment), an estimated duration in days, and the type of maintenance (selected from a predefined list including at minimum: standard revisjon, lekkasje, periodisk tilsyn, turbinbytte, and a free-text "annet" option). The driftsleder also indicates whether the stop is flexible in timing, using a three-value selector: ikke flyttbar (cannot be moved — the stop must happen now for technical or safety reasons), kan flyttes (can be moved within the desired period), or høy fleksibilitet (highly flexible — Energihandel has broad latitude to suggest the best market timing).

The third group captures constraints and context: a criticality level (lav, middels, høy) that tells Energihandel how urgently the stop needs to happen, a dependency field indicating whether external parties are involved (e.g. "Ekstern leverandør", "Internt mannskap only"), and an optional free-text comment field where the driftsleder can add anything that doesn't fit the structured fields — for example, a note that a specific contractor is only available in a certain week, or that the stop is linked to a regulatory deadline.

All fields except the comment are required. The form validates before submission and prevents submission if required fields are missing, surfacing clear field-level feedback. The driftsleder can freely edit any field before submitting.

When the driftsleder submits the form, the request is saved with a unique ID, a timestamp, the submitting user's identity, and an initial status of "Ny". The driftsleder immediately sees a confirmation screen. The confirmation shows a compact summary of the submitted request — the key fields only, not a full form replay: the generated request ID, stasjon/aggregat, ønsket periode, varighet, and status. Below the summary, a message confirms that Energihandel has been notified and will come back with proposed stop windows. The driftsleder cannot edit the submitted request from this screen — if changes are needed, this version of the feature does not support editing; the driftsleder would need to contact Energihandel directly. This constraint is intentional for v1 and should not be worked around.

The confirmation screen also shows a list of the driftsleder's own previous requests — their personal request history — with each entry showing stasjon, aggregat, type vedlikehold, elapsed time since submission, and current status badge. This list lets the driftsleder track where their open requests stand in the workflow without needing to contact anyone. The status values visible in this list span the full lifecycle of a request: Ny (just submitted, awaiting Energihandel), Under behandling (Energihandel is analyzing it), Alternativer sendt (Energihandel has sent back proposed windows, action needed from Drift), and Besluttet (a timing has been confirmed by Drift). Only the driftsleder's own requests appear in this list — not requests from other driftsledere at other plants.

The status "Ny" is set automatically on submission and cannot be set manually. All subsequent status transitions are driven by Energihandel's actions in later features — this feature only ever creates requests in status "Ny".

The Drift registration form establishes the core data entity — the Vedlikeholdsforespørsel (maintenance stop request) — that the entire downstream workflow is built on. The entity must carry: request ID (unique, system-generated), submission timestamp, submitting user identity, stasjon, aggregat (with its associated MW capacity), ønsket startperiode, estimert varighet (days), type vedlikehold, fleksibilitet (three-value enum), kritikalitet (three-value enum), avhengighet, kommentar (nullable), and status. The status field is the primary coordination mechanism between Drift and Energihandel across the full workflow — its lifecycle must be designed correctly from the start.

Explicitly out of scope for this feature: Energihandel's view of incoming requests, the analysis tool, proposal creation and delivery, Drift's selection of a proposed window, the final confirmed-decision screen, any editing of submitted requests, any deletion of requests, attachment or document upload, and any integration with external systems (ISY, BRREG, Nord Pool, Volue). The notification to Energihandel that triggers when a request is submitted is a consequence of this feature but its implementation details (channel, format) belong to the notification feature or are handled by the platform's automation layer — not by this form spec.

Success: a driftsleder with no prior training can complete and submit a maintenance stop request in under two minutes. The confirmation screen gives them immediate certainty that their request has been received and Energihandel has been notified. The personal request history on the confirmation screen means the driftsleder never needs to call or email to ask "where does my request stand?"
